\section*{Implementation}

Everything is written from scratch in Python~3.12 with NumPy for linear algebra and Matplotlib
for plots; no fitting library is used. The code, the data and every output are at
\begin{center}\url{https://github.com/Chaitanya-Keyal/polynomial-regression}\end{center}
\texttt{src/p1.py} to \texttt{src/p4.py} hold one problem each, \texttt{src/common/} the shared
bases and solvers, and \texttt{python -m src.run} regenerates every figure and every result in
this report from the six provided CSV files, which are loaded as given. $\sin(2\pi x)$ appears
only in plots. The random seed for the per-epoch shuffling in P1 is $\res{p1-seed}$ (a fresh
\texttt{np.random.default\_rng(\res{p1-seed})} per run), so a rerun reproduces every number
exactly. The few values that rounding alone determines (the converged $E$ near $10^{-30}$ and the
gaps between GD and the closed form in P1 and P4) are printed to one significant figure, since
another linear-algebra library can change their leading digit. No explicit matrix inverse is
formed: least squares uses the SVD-based
\texttt{np.linalg.lstsq}, and ridge and the Bayesian posterior use \texttt{np.linalg.solve}.
RMSE is $\sqrt{\text{mean squared error}}$ throughout.

\section{P1: Closed form vs.\ the gradient descent family}

\paragraph{Method.}
The degree-$d$ model $y(x,\w) = \sum_{j=0}^{d} w_j x^j$ has design matrix
$\Phib \in \mathbb{R}^{N\times(d+1)}$ with rows $[1, x_n, \dots, x_n^d]$ and objective
$E(\w) = \frac12\norm{\Phib\w - \tb}^2$. The closed-form minimiser solves
$\Phib^\T\Phib\w = \Phib^\T\tb$, but for $d = 9$ we have $\mathrm{cond}(\Phib) = \res{p1-cond-phi}$,
and forming $\Phib^\T\Phib$ would square that to about $10^{14}$. We therefore solve the least
squares problem directly with \texttt{np.linalg.lstsq}, which works through the SVD of $\Phib$.

For gradient descent we take the thin QR decomposition $\Phib = QR$, set $\bm\alpha = R\w$ and
minimise $E(\bm\alpha) = \frac12\norm{Q\bm\alpha - \tb}^2$ from $\bm\alpha = \mathbf 0$, then recover
$\w = R^{-1}\bm\alpha$ by a solve with $R$ ($\mathrm{cond}(Q) = \res{p1-cond-q}$).
With $\mathbf q_n^\T$ the $n$-th row of $Q$ and $r_n = \mathbf q_n^\T\bm\alpha - t_n$ its residual,
one epoch is
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Batch GD}: one step $\bm\alpha \leftarrow \bm\alpha - \eta\, Q^\T(Q\bm\alpha - \tb)$,
        the full sum, not divided by $N$;
  \item \textbf{Stochastic GD}: shuffle, then for each $n$ in turn
        $\bm\alpha \leftarrow \bm\alpha - \eta\, r_n \mathbf q_n$ ($N = 10$ updates);
  \item \textbf{Mini-batch GD}: shuffle, then for each batch $B$ of the shuffled order (sizes
        3, 3, 3, 1) $\bm\alpha \leftarrow \bm\alpha - \frac{\eta}{|B|}\sum_{n\in B} r_n\mathbf q_n$
        (4 updates).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Closed-form fits.} Figure~\ref{fig:p1-fits} shows the least-squares fits for
$d = 1,\dots,9$ and Table~\ref{tab:p1-closed} their training error. The models are nested, so
the training error can only fall as $d$ grows; at $d = 9$ there are as many weights as points
and the polynomial interpolates the data ($E \approx 0$). Degrees 1 and 2 underfit, degrees 3
to 6 follow $\sin(2\pi x)$ closely, and from $d = 7$ on the curve starts to bend between the
points to chase the noise, clearly so at $d = 8$ and 9 near the ends of $[0, 1]$.

\paragraph{Learning-rate study.} In the QR coordinates the Hessian of $E$ is $Q^\T Q = I$, so
every eigenvalue is 1 and Batch GD gives $\bm\alpha_k - \bm\alpha^\star = (1-\eta)^k(\bm\alpha_0 -
\bm\alpha^\star)$. Since $Q$ is square here ($N = d + 1$), the minimum is $E(\bm\alpha^\star) = 0$,
so $E_k = (1-\eta)^{2k}E_0$ with $E_0 = \frac12\norm{\tb}^2 = \res{p1-E0}$.
GD converges exactly when $|1-\eta| < 1$, i.e.\ $0 < \eta < 2$, fastest at $\eta = 1$ (one epoch).
Figure~\ref{fig:p1-lr} shows the three regimes over 100 epochs:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\eta = \res{p1-lr-small-eta}$ (too small): $E$ is multiplied by only
        $0.99^2 \approx 0.98$ per epoch and is still $\res{p1-lr-small-E100}$ after 100 epochs;
  \item $\eta = \res{p1-lr-good-eta}$ (well chosen): $E$ falls by a factor of 4 per epoch and
        reaches the floating-point floor within about 50 epochs ($E_{100} = \res{p1-lr-good-E100}$);
  \item $\eta = \res{p1-lr-large-eta}$ (too large): $|1-\eta| = 1.1$, so the error flips sign and
        grows by a factor of $1.1$ every epoch. After 100 epochs $E = \res{p1-lr-large-E100} = 1.1^{200}E_0$,
        and $\bm\alpha_{100} = (1 - 1.1^{100})\bm\alpha^\star$, so the weight vector is
        $\res{p1-lr-ratio}$ times the closed-form solution (Table~\ref{tab:p1-w}).
\end{itemize}
At exactly $\eta = 2$ the factor is $-1$: $\bm\alpha$ alternates between $\mathbf 0$ and
$2\bm\alpha^\star$, each step overshooting the minimum by exactly as much as it started from it,
and $E$ stays at $E_0$ forever. It neither converges nor diverges.

error of only $\res{p1-batch-rel}$: all three variants agree with the closed form to the
precision the problem allows. The error is not optimisation error: GD converges to the QR
solution $R^{-1}Q^\T\tb$ to within rounding, which is why the three variants' errors agree, and
what remains
is the gap between two direct solutions of the same system (QR, and the SVD inside
\texttt{lstsq}), well inside the relative error of about
$\mathrm{cond}(\Phib)\,\epsilon_{\mathrm{mach}} \approx 3\times10^{-9}$ that rounding allows. The
final $E(\bm\alpha)$ is measured in the QR coordinates; recomputed as
$\frac12\norm{\Phib\w - \tb}^2$ from the recovered $\w$ it is of order $10^{-22}$ for every
variant, as for the closed form in Table~\ref{tab:p1-closed}, because each entry of $\Phib\w$ is a
sum of terms as large as $7\times10^4$ that cancel.

\paragraph{Discussion.} In Figure~\ref{fig:p1-loss} the SGD curve lies on the Batch curve epoch
for epoch (relative gap below $\res{p1-sgd-gap}$ while $E > 10^{-10}$) until both reach the
rounding floor, while Mini-batch GD is clearly slower. (i)~With $N = d + 1 = 10$ the thin $Q$ is
square and orthogonal, so its \emph{rows} $\mathbf q_n$ are orthonormal: an SGD step on point
$n$ multiplies $r_n$ by $(1-\eta)$ and leaves every other residual untouched
($\mathbf q_m^\T\mathbf q_n = 0$), so one pass in any order multiplies every residual by
$(1-\eta)$ once, exactly like one Batch step $\mathbf r \leftarrow (I - \eta QQ^\T)\mathbf r =
(1-\eta)\mathbf r$. (ii)~Mini-batch GD \emph{averages} over its batch, so the nine points in
batches of three shrink only by $(1-\eta/3)$ per epoch (the singleton by $1-\eta$): $E$ shrinks
by a factor of about $\res{p1-minibatch-factor}$ per epoch instead of $0.81$, and Mini-batch
needs about 2.6 times as many epochs ($\res{p1-minibatch-epochs12}$ vs.\
$\res{p1-batch-epochs12}$).

\section{P2: Ridge regression as a MAP estimate}

\paragraph{MAP $\Rightarrow$ ridge.} By Bayes' rule $p(\w\mid\tb) \propto p(\tb\mid\w)\,p(\w)$ with
$p(\tb\mid\w) \propto \exp(-\frac\beta2\norm{\Phib\w - \tb}^2)$ and
$p(\w) \propto \exp(-\frac\alpha2\norm{\w}^2)$. Taking $-\log$ and dividing by $\beta$, maximising
the posterior is minimising
\[ \tilde E(\w) = \tfrac12\norm{\Phib\w - \tb}^2 + \tfrac\lambda2\norm{\w}^2, \qquad \lambda = \alpha/\beta . \]
Setting $\nabla\tilde E = \Phib^\T(\Phib\w - \tb) + \lambda\w = \mathbf 0$ gives
$(\Phib^\T\Phib + \lambda I)\w = \Phib^\T\tb$. For $\lambda > 0$ the matrix is symmetric positive
definite, so the solution is unique; we solve the system directly for every $\lambda$ in the grid
(49 values from $10^{-10}$ to $10^{2}$), in the $d = 9$ monomial basis of P1.

\paragraph{GD at $\lambda = \res{p2-gd-lam}$.} Plain batch GD in the monomial basis,
$\w \leftarrow \w - \eta\,(\Phib^\T(\Phib\w - \tb) + \lambda\w)$ from $\w = \mathbf 0$ with
$\eta = \res{p2-gd-eta}$, reached $\norm{\w_{\mathrm{GD}} - \wc}/\norm{\wc} < 10^{-6}$ after
$\res{p2-gd-iters}$ iterations (Figure~\ref{fig:p2-gd}); its final objective
$\res{p2-gd-obj}$ matches the closed form's $\res{p2-gd-obj-closed}$. Here
$\mathrm{cond}(\Phib^\T\Phib) = \res{p2-cond-before}$ and
$\mathrm{cond}(\Phib^\T\Phib + \lambda I) = \res{p2-cond-after}$: the penalty adds $\lambda$ to
every eigenvalue, lifting the smallest from almost zero to about $\lambda$ and cutting the
condition number by eleven orders of magnitude, which is what makes plain GD in this basis
practical.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering\fig[0.95\linewidth]{p2_gd}
  \caption{Batch GD on the ridge objective at $\lambda = \res{p2-gd-lam}$: $\tilde E(\w)$ per
    iteration (left) and the relative distance to the closed-form solution (right).}
  \label{fig:p2-gd}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{RMSE sweep and $\lambda^\star$.} Figure~\ref{fig:p2-rmse} shows train and test RMSE
over the grid. The test RMSE is smallest at $\lambda^\star = \res{p2-best-lam}$, where it is
$\res{p2-best-test}$ (train $\res{p2-best-train}$). It is within $1.05\times$ its minimum on the
$\res{p2-flat-n}$ contiguous grid points $\lambda \in [\res{p2-flat-lo}, \res{p2-flat-hi}]$
around $\lambda^\star$, which is the interval we report; after peaking at
$\res{p2-flat-peak-ratio}\times$ the minimum at $\lambda = \res{p2-flat-peak-lam}$ it dips back
under $1.05\times$ at the isolated grid point $\lambda = \res{p2-flat-others}$, so the qualifying
set is not a single interval.

\paragraph{Discussion.} The minimum is flat: the test RMSE stays within 5\% of its minimum over
almost two decades of $\lambda$ around $\lambda^\star$, and within 7\% from $\res{p2-flat-lo}$ to
$\res{p2-flat-others}$, so the exact choice of $\lambda$ in this range hardly matters. Below the
interval the penalty is too weak and the polynomial chases the noise (train RMSE
$\res{p2-overfit-train}$, test $\res{p2-overfit-test}$ at $\lambda = \res{p2-overfit-lam}$);
above it the test RMSE plateaus near 0.12 until $\lambda \approx 10^{-4}$, after which the
over-shrunk weights underfit and train and test RMSE rise together (to $\res{p2-underfit-test}$
at $\lambda = \res{p2-underfit-lam}$).

of magnitude from the overfit to the underfit model: the penalty buys smoothness by forbidding
the large, cancelling coefficients an interpolating polynomial needs.

\section{P3: L1-regularised regression via subgradient descent}

\paragraph{Method.} With $u = 2x - 1$, the design matrix is $\Phi_{nj} = P_j(u_n)$,
$j = 0, \dots, 9$ (NumPy's \texttt{legvander} applied to $2x - 1$). It spans the same
degree-9 polynomials as the monomial basis, but $\mathrm{cond}(\Phib^\T\Phib) = \res{p3-cond}$
instead of $\res{p2-cond-before}$ for the monomials (Section~2). Subgradient descent on
$E(\w) = \frac12\norm{\Phib\w - \tb}^2 + \lambda\norm{\w}_1$ runs
$\w \leftarrow \w - \eta\,(\Phib^\T(\Phib\w - \tb) + \lambda\,\mathrm{sign}(\w))$ with
$\mathrm{sign}(0) = 0$ and $\eta = \res{p3-eta}$ from $\w = \mathbf 0$ for $\res{p3-iters}$
iterations at each of the 21 values of $\lambda$ from $10^{-4}$ to $1$. Every reported quantity
comes from the final iterate, and a coefficient counts as $\approx 0$ when $|w_j| < 2\eta\lambda$,
since with a constant step a zero coefficient chatters around 0 with amplitude about
$\eta\lambda$.

\paragraph{Convergence and verification at $\lambda = \res{p3-check-lam}$.}
Figure~\ref{fig:p3-conv} shows $E$ and the best $E$ so far. Subgradient descent is not a descent
method: once near the optimum, the $\mathrm{sign}$ term flips for the coefficients at zero and $E$
rises and falls by about $10^{-3}$ (right panel), while the best-so-far $E$ steps down to about
$10^{-4}$ above $E^\star$ and then levels off: with a constant step it cannot close that gap.
The final iterate has $E = \res{p3-check-E}$, against the reference
$E^\star = \res{p3-check-Eref}$, and
\begin{align*}
  \w &= \res{p3-check-w},\\
  \w^\star &= \res{p3-check-wref},
\end{align*}
so $\norm{\w - \w^\star}_2 = \res{p3-check-wdiff}$, the size of the $\eta\lambda$ chatter. The
optimality conditions hold to within that chatter (a step of $\eta\lambda$ in one coefficient
moves $\Phib^\T\mathbf r$ by up to $\lambda_{\max}(\Phib^\T\Phib)\,\eta\lambda \approx 0.06$):
with $\mathbf r = \tb - \Phib\w$, over the
$\res{p3-opt-nactive}$ coefficients that are not $\approx 0$,
$\max_j |\Phib_j^\T\mathbf r - \lambda\,\mathrm{sign}(w_j)| = \res{p3-opt-active}$, and over the
$\res{p3-opt-nzero}$ that are, $\max_j |\Phib_j^\T\mathbf r|/\lambda = \res{p3-opt-zero} \le 1$.

\paragraph{Discussion.} L1 produces exact zeros because the subgradient of $|w_j|$ at 0 is the
whole interval $[-1, 1]$: $w_j = 0$ is optimal whenever $|\Phib_j^\T\mathbf r| \le \lambda$, so a
feature whose correlation with the residual stays below $\lambda$ is switched off entirely, and as
$\lambda$ grows the coefficients cross that threshold one after another (Figure~\ref{fig:p3-path},
Table~\ref{tab:p3-zeros}). The L2 gradient $\lambda w_j$ vanishes at 0, so Ridge pulls each
coefficient toward zero in proportion to its size and never gets there; geometrically, the SSE
contours meet the $\ell_1$ ball at a corner on an axis but the round $\ell_2$ ball away from the
axes. Table~\ref{tab:p3-vs} shows the result: at almost the same $\norm{\w}_2$, the Lasso has
$\res{p3-lasso-nsmall}$ coefficients below 0.01 and Ridge only $\res{p3-ridge-nsmall}$. The first
coefficients to go carry almost no signal ($\sin(2\pi x) = -\sin(\pi u)$ has sizeable Legendre
coefficients only on $P_1$, $P_3$ and $P_5$), so up to $\lambda^\star$ the Lasso mostly removes
noise and its test RMSE falls; past $\lambda^\star$ it shrinks the signal coefficients $w_1$,
$w_3$ and $w_5$, and train and test RMSE rise together as bias takes over.

\section{P4: Bayesian regression}

\paragraph{Method.} With $\bm\phi(x) = [1, x, x^2, x^3]^\T$, prior $\mathcal N(\mathbf 0,
\alpha^{-1}I)$ and noise precision $\beta$, the posterior is $\mathcal N(\w\mid\mathbf m_N, S_N)$
with $S_N^{-1} = \alpha I + \beta\Phib^\T\Phib$ and $\mathbf m_N = \beta S_N\Phib^\T\tb$, and the
predictive distribution is $\mathcal N(m(x), s^2(x))$ with $m(x) = \bm\phi(x)^\T\mathbf m_N$ and
$s^2(x) = \beta^{-1} + \bm\phi(x)^\T S_N\bm\phi(x)$. We use $\alpha = \res{p4-alpha}$ and
$\beta = 1/0.01 = \res{p4-beta}$ for every $N$. $S_N$ is never formed: with
$A = S_N^{-1}$, both $\mathbf m_N = \beta A^{-1}\Phib^\T\tb$ and $\bm\phi^\T S_N\bm\phi =
\bm\phi^\T A^{-1}\bm\phi$ come from linear solves with $A$.

\paragraph{Equivalence.} Since $\mathbf m_N = \beta(\alpha I + \beta\Phib^\T\Phib)^{-1}\Phib^\T\tb
= (\Phib^\T\Phib + \tfrac\alpha\beta I)^{-1}\Phib^\T\tb$, the posterior mean is exactly the ridge
solution with $\lambda = \alpha/\beta = \res{p4-lam}$; a Gaussian posterior's mean is also its
mode, so MAP and posterior mean coincide, and so do $\w_{\mathrm{MAP}}^\T\bm\phi(x)$ and $m(x)$.
Numerically, for $N = 10$, $\mathbf m_N = \res{p4-mN}$, $\w_{\mathrm{MAP}} = \res{p4-wmap}$ and
$\norm{\w_{\mathrm{MAP}} - \mathbf m_N}_2 = \res{p4-diff}$: the two differ only by rounding, and
their curves coincide in Figure~\ref{fig:p4-pred}.

\paragraph{Discussion.} Every training point adds $\beta\bm\phi(x_n)\bm\phi(x_n)^\T$ to the
posterior precision $S_N^{-1}$, so the posterior covariance $S_N$, and with it the parameter
uncertainty $\bm\phi(x)^\T S_N\bm\phi(x)$, shrinks as $N$ grows: roughly like $1/N$ in the middle
of $[0, 1]$, more slowly at the ends, where a cubic is least pinned down and the band stays widest
(averaged over $x$, its share of the predictive variance falls from $\res{p4-width-5-share}\%$
at $N = 5$ to $\res{p4-width-50-share}\%$ at $N = 50$). The observation-noise term $\beta^{-1}$ does not depend
on the data at all: it is the scatter of a new target around the true function, so no amount of
data removes it, and the band can only approach the floor $2\times1.96\,\beta^{-1/2} =
\res{p4-floor}$, never cross it.
